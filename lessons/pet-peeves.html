Grammar Complaining about annoying habits
Five ways to sound really annoyed
always / constantly / forever + Present ContinuousHe’s always leaving his socks on the sofa. (= again and again, and it annoys me)
keep (on) + -ingShe keeps sending me voice messages.
will / would (stressed) for typical habitsHe WILL talk during films! · My ex would always be late.
I wish + would / wouldn’tI wish people would let others off the train first. · I wish he wouldn’t chew so loudly.
Cleft sentences (to emphasise)What really gets me is when people … · The thing that drives me mad is … · It’s not the noise that bothers me, it’s …

⚠️ I wish he would … = I want another person to change. Not about yourself: I wish I would → I wish I could / I wish I didn’t …

5A Watch a funny ‘investigation’ into one modern pet peeve: speakerphone in public. Answer the questions.

▶ Petty Misdemeanors · The Person Who Uses Speakerphone in Public (2026) 6:53 open on YouTube

1When, according to the narrator, does speakerphone make perfect sense?

At home / in private, when your hands are busy (cooking, folding laundry).

2Why is it ‘no longer eavesdropping’ when someone uses speakerphone in public?

Eavesdropping takes effort; here the information ‘finds you’ — you hear both people without trying.

3What is the ‘acoustic arms race’?

The caller speaks louder because the room is noisy, the phone gets louder, and then other people in the room start speaking louder too.

4Why can’t you simply ask the person to turn it down?

You would become part of a conversation you’re trying hard not to join (and you can’t always move away or use headphones).

5Why is the person on the other end of the call ‘the most complicated participant’?

They often don’t know they’re on speaker, so they share private things that are broadcast to strangers.

6What exceptions does the narrator accept?

Busy hands, accessibility features, urgent calls, a broken earpiece, someone else needs to hear, a 10-second call in an empty space.

7What is the ‘strangest rule’ of all?

The audience must listen but is not allowed to take part (you can’t turn around and give your opinion).

8What simple ‘policy’ does the narrator suggest?

In shared indoor spaces, for longer calls: hold the phone normally, use headphones, step outside or find a quiet corner.

Softeners for a difficult conversation

I don’t know if you’ve noticed, but … · This might sound a bit petty, but … · It’s probably just me, but … · No big deal, but … · Would you mind (not) + -ing …? · I was wondering if you could … · Could we maybe …? · I’d really appreciate it if you could …

C Rewrite these complaints so that they sound diplomatic.

1. Stop leaving your dishes in the sink!
I don’t know if you’ve noticed, but the dishes have been in the sink since Monday. Could we take turns washing up?
2. You’re always late. It’s so rude.
This might sound a bit petty, but could you message me if you’re running late? I end up waiting for ages.
3. Your speakerphone is driving everyone mad.
Sorry to bother you — would you mind taking the call off speaker? It’s a bit loud in here.
4. Why do you keep sending me voice messages?!
It’s probably just me, but I find texts easier to read at work. Could you write instead of sending voice notes?
