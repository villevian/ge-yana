Forget the apps — join a run club

An adapted article · 2025 data from Strava and Brooks Running

Ten years ago, a running club meant serious athletes in old T-shirts, timing every kilometre. Today, the picture is very different. On a Wednesday evening in London, New York or Kyiv, you can see crowds of people in bright trainers jogging slowly through the city — and chatting the whole way.

According to the fitness app Strava, participation in running clubs grew by 59% in 2024, and 58% of people in its survey said they had made new friends through a fitness group. In the UK, a study by Brooks Running found that 63% of run club members had made new friends there.

So why now? What many members say is that it is simply hard to make friends as an adult. You finish university, you move to a new city, you work from home — and suddenly your social life is a group chat. What a run club offers is a reason to leave the house, a regular time and a group of people who are happy to see you.

The other thing that has made run clubs popular is social media. Videos of runners heading to a bar after a 5K have gone viral on TikTok, and some clubs in New York now advertise themselves as an alternative to dating apps. One of them, Lunge Run Club, became so popular that it created its own app.

Not everyone is a fan. Some long-time runners complain that the new clubs are more about selfies and drinks than training. But for most new members, the pace is not the point. The thing they love is that nobody cares how fast you are — you just turn up.

1Modern run clubs are mainly for serious athletes.
2Strava says run club participation went up by more than half in 2024.
3Most UK run club members in the Brooks study made new friends.
4The writer thinks remote work makes it easier to make friends.
5TikTok videos helped run clubs become popular.
6All experienced runners love the new clubs.
